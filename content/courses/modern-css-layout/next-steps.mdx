## Keep building in the playground

Everything here also runs in the full-page
[HTML/CSS/JS playground](/playground/web), with multi-file tabs and a
live preview. Rebuild any lesson's example from scratch, or start
something new.

Project ideas that stretch these skills:

1. **A pricing page**, three plan cards in a responsive grid, one
   "featured" plan scaled up with `transform`, all colors from custom
   properties.
2. **A photo gallery**, `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit,
   minmax(...))` with a hover zoom, no media queries needed.
3. **A dark-mode toggle**, define your palette as variables, then flip
   them with a `data-theme` attribute and a few lines of JavaScript.

## Go deeper on modern CSS

You already have the modern kit: container queries, `:has()`, nesting,
logical properties, `aspect-ratio`, and `grid-template-areas` all
appeared in the lessons. Here's what sits just past the edge of this
course:

- **Cascade layers** (`@layer`), declare a priority order for whole
  groups of rules, so a reset can never accidentally out-specify a
  component. It is the cascade lesson applied at codebase scale.
- **`subgrid`**, let a nested grid inherit its parent's tracks so that
  cards in a row line their headings and footers up with each other, no
  matter how much text each one holds.
- **`@property`**, give a custom property a real type. A variable
  declared as `<angle>` or `<color>` can be *transitioned*, which is how
  you animate a gradient's angle rather than cross-fading two images.
- **Scroll-driven animations** (`animation-timeline: scroll()` and
  `view()`), tie a keyframe animation to scroll position instead of
  time. Progress bars and reveal-on-scroll effects, no JavaScript.
- **View transitions**, animate between two states, or two whole pages,
  by letting the browser cross-fade the before and after for you.
- **`color-mix()` and `oklch()`**, build a palette by mixing and
  lightening one brand color instead of hand-picking ten hex codes.
  Pairs directly with the custom-properties lesson.

## The habits that matter

1. **Keep specificity low and even.** Lean on classes; you can always
   override with one more.
2. **`box-sizing: border-box` everywhere.** Then `width` means width.
3. **Fluid first, breakpoints second.** Reach for a media query only
   when the layout must change *shape*.

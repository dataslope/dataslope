Until around 2024, improving a model meant training a bigger one. The
shift since then has been toward spending more compute **at inference
time**, and it produced a distinct category of model.

## The mechanism

A reasoning model generates a long internal sequence before its
answer: working, false starts, checks, revisions. Those are
**thinking tokens**. They are generated by exactly the same next-token
mechanism as everything else. Nothing new was added to the
architecture.

<Figure
  slug="llm-reasoning-models-inline-cutout"
  alt="A machine that lays down a long line of working chips on a side bench before placing its final answer chip"
/>

What changed is training. These models are trained with reinforcement
learning against **verifiable** outcomes, mathematics problems with
known answers, code that either passes tests or does not. The reward
is for reaching a correct final answer, so behaviors that help
(checking work, trying another approach, backtracking) get reinforced.

An end-of-thinking marker separates the working from the answer, and
the working is usually hidden or summarized rather than shown.

<CodeBlock
  adapter="python"
  files={[
    {
      filename: "main.py",
      starterCode: `# The shape of a reasoning model's output. The thinking section is
# generated the same way as the answer, just kept separate.
transcript = """<thinking>
The question asks for compound growth over 3 years at 8%.
First attempt: 1000 * 3 * 0.08 = 240. That is simple interest,
not compound. Let me redo it.
Compound: 1000 * 1.08^3 = 1000 * 1.259712 = 1259.71
Check: year 1 -> 1080, year 2 -> 1166.40, year 3 -> 1259.71. Matches.
</thinking>
After three years the balance is $1,259.71."""

thinking, answer = transcript.split("</thinking>")
thinking_tokens = len(thinking.split()) * 1.3
answer_tokens = len(answer.split()) * 1.3

print(f"thinking: ~{thinking_tokens:.0f} tokens (hidden, usually billed)")
print(f"answer:   ~{answer_tokens:.0f} tokens (shown)")
print(f"ratio:     {thinking_tokens / answer_tokens:.1f}x more thinking than answer")
print()
print("Note the model caught its own error mid-working.")
print("That self-correction is the behavior reinforcement training rewards.")
`,
    },
  ]}
/>

Notice the model catching its own mistake in that transcript. That is
the whole value proposition, and it only works because the training
rewarded reaching the right answer rather than sounding right along the
way.

## Diminishing returns are steep

More thinking helps, and it stops helping much sooner than you would
guess. The first several hundred thinking tokens buy a great deal. Going
from 10,000 to 11,000 buys very little on most problems.

  alt="A ramp that rises steeply then flattens almost level, working chips piling up along its flat stretch"
/>

    {
      filename: "main.py",

# Stylized accuracy-versus-thinking curve. The saturating shape is the
# consistently reported finding; the exact numbers vary by task.
budgets = np.array([0, 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000, 16000])
accuracy = np.array([0.42, 0.58, 0.66, 0.72, 0.76, 0.78, 0.79, 0.79])

print(f"{'thinking':>9} {'accuracy':>9} {'gain':>7}  per 1k tokens")
prev_acc, prev_b = accuracy[0], budgets[0]
for b, a in zip(budgets, accuracy):
    if b == 0:
        print(f"{b:>9} {a:>9.2f} {'-':>7}")
        continue
    gain = a - prev_acc
    per_k = gain / ((b - prev_b) / 1000)
    print(f"{b:>9} {a:>9.2f} {gain:>+7.3f}  {per_k:+.4f}")
    prev_acc, prev_b = a, b

print()
print("The last doubling bought nothing and cost 8,000 tokens.")
`,
    },
  ]}
/>

## Where it helps and where it does not

**Helps a lot:** multi-step mathematics, competitive programming,
formal logic, planning under constraints, debugging. The common
property is a **verifiable intermediate structure**, where checking
your own work is meaningful.

**Helps little or not at all:** factual recall (thinking longer about
a fact you do not have does not produce it), style and tone,
translation, simple extraction, summarization. Thinking about the
capital of France does not improve the answer.

There is also a documented **overthinking** failure, where extended
reasoning on a simple problem talks itself out of a correct initial
answer. More compute is not monotonically better.

## What it costs

Thinking tokens are output tokens. They are billed at the output rate,
which is typically five to six times the input rate, and they are
usually not shown to you. A reasoning-heavy response can cost several
times a direct one for the same visible text.

Providers expose a control over this: an *effort* level, a thinking
budget, or a distinct model tier. The specific parameter names change
frequently, but the concept is stable, and the right default for most
production work is **the lowest setting that passes your evaluation**.

<Callout type="warn" title="A common and expensive mistake">
Running every request at maximum reasoning effort because it scores
best on benchmarks. Most production traffic is classification,
extraction, and formatting, none of which benefit. Route by task:
reasoning effort where the task has verifiable structure, minimal
effort everywhere else.
</Callout>

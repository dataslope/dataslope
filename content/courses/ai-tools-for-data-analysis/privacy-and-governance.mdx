Every chapter so far has been about correctness. This one is about a
category of mistake that no amount of verification catches, because
the damage is done at the moment you press enter.

## What actually happens when you paste

The text goes to the provider's servers and is processed there. Beyond
that, the specifics depend entirely on which product and which tier
you are using, and they differ substantially:

* **Consumer free tiers** have historically been the most permissive
  about using conversations to improve models, usually with an opt-out
  setting that many users never find.
* **Paid consumer tiers** generally offer stronger commitments and
  clearer controls.
* **Business, team, and enterprise tiers** typically commit contractually
  to not training on your data, and offer retention controls and audit
  logging.
* **API access** is generally covered by a separate agreement from the
  chat products, often with different defaults.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check, do not assume">
These arrangements change, and they differ per product and per region.
Read your organization's actual agreement rather than relying on a
general impression, including one from this course. The categories
above are a map of what to look for, not a statement of any vendor's
current terms.
</Callout>

## The four questions worth answering once

Answer these for your organization and you have covered most of the
ground:

1. **Which assistant is approved, at which tier?** Approval usually
   attaches to a specific plan, not to the product name.
2. **Is training on your inputs disabled?** Under the agreement, not
   just in a settings toggle.
3. **What is the retention period?** How long do the conversations
   persist, and who can read them?
4. **What data classes are prohibited?** Almost every organization has
   a list, and it typically includes personal data, health data,
   payment data, credentials, and unreleased financials.

## The practical rule for analysts

Most analysis does not need real values.

* **Schema over data.** Column names and types are usually enough to
  get correct code. You rarely need the rows.
* **Synthetic samples.** If you need example values, make some up with
  the same shape. `customer_042` and `2025-03-14` teach the model the
  format without exposing anyone.
* **Aggregate over individual.** "Revenue by region" is far less
  sensitive than a row-level export.
* **Never paste credentials.** Connection strings, API keys, and
  tokens should never enter a chat window, and this is worth
  a hard rule rather than a judgment call.

Applying these gets you nearly all the benefit at a fraction of the
exposure, which is why they are worth doing even where policy would
permit more.

## The connected-tool wrinkle

Connecting an assistant to a live database changes the question. Now
the data flowing to the provider is not what you chose to paste; it is
whatever the assistant decides to query. Your controls become:

* **Read-only credentials**, always.
* **Scoped access** to the specific schemas needed, not the whole
  warehouse.
* **Views rather than base tables** where sensitive columns can be
  excluded at the source.
* **Query logging**, so you can review what was actually accessed.

Data Analyst interviews lean heavily on **SQL** and on whether you can
translate a vague business question into the right query and a clear
answer. Expect a take-home or live SQL screen, a few spreadsheet/stats
questions, and a "how would you analyze X?" discussion.

## What interviewers probe

- **SQL fluency**, `JOIN`s, `GROUP BY`, filtering with `WHERE` vs `HAVING`, `CASE` expressions, and basic window functions.
- **Aggregation & framing**, picking the right grain, handling NULLs, and avoiding double-counting after joins.
- **Communication**, stating assumptions, caveats, and what you'd check next.
- **Tools**, spreadsheets (pivot tables, lookups) and a BI tool (Tableau, Power BI, Looker).

## How AI has changed this interview

- **Checking beats writing.** Expect "an assistant wrote this query; is it right?" and take-home reviews that walk through every filter and join, because a plausible query is now cheap to produce.
- **Responsible use.** What you would paste into an AI tool, how you disclose using one, and how you keep hallucinated insights out of a report.
- **AI features in BI tools.** Natural-language questions only get correct answers on top of clean, well-defined metrics, which is increasingly the analyst's job to provide.
- **AI-on and AI-off rounds.** The live SQL screen is usually AI-off, so fluency still matters. Ask the recruiter about each round.

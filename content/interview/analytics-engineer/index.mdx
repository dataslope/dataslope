Analytics Engineer sits between data engineering and analytics: you own
the **transformation layer**, turning raw warehouse tables into clean,
tested, documented models (usually with **dbt**). Interviews test SQL,
dimensional modeling, and software practices applied to analytics.

## What interviewers probe

- **SQL & modeling**, dimensional modeling (facts & dimensions), star schemas, grain, and slowly-changing dimensions.
- **dbt**, models, refs, sources, tests, materializations (view / table / incremental), and documentation.
- **Software practice**, version control, CI, modularity (staging → intermediate → marts), and DRY SQL.
- **Trade-offs**, when to materialize as a table vs view vs incremental model, and cost/freshness balance.

## Sample concept check

<MultipleChoice
  markdown={`In a star schema, a table storing the **measurable events** (e.g. one row per order line, with quantities and amounts) is called what?

- A dimension table.
  > Dimension tables hold descriptive context (customer, product, date) that you filter and group *by*, not the measurements themselves.
- [o] A fact table.
  > Fact tables store the measurable events at a defined grain, with numeric measures and foreign keys out to the dimensions.
- A staging model.
  > "Staging" describes a dbt layer (lightly cleaned source data), not a star-schema table role.
- A surrogate key.
  > A surrogate key is a synthetic identifier column, not a kind of table.`}
/>
